Observations (findings in the IIA standards) record deficiencies or gaps. Observations tend to stem from tests by default and reference the test that revealed them. Observations can also be created beyond testing (E.g. design deficiencies, missing control, something surfacing in a walkthrough). In these cases, they attach directly to the engagement.

An observation can cover multiple exceptions stemming from the same cause: findings sharing a root cause are written as **one** observation with a broader condition, not scattered into several.

## The four-part finding

Every observation is built from four fields that read as a chain: **Criteria** and **Condition** establish the gap, **Root Cause** explains it, **Effect** justifies the rating.

| Field | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Condition** | What was found. Facts of the observation: what is happening, where, how often, with quantities where testing supports them. "Reviews were not performed" is a condition; "access controls are weak" is an opinion. |
| **Criteria** | The expected outcome, with the specific source cited. This could be policy name and section, procedure, regulation, contract or something similar. To be GIAS compliant these should be specific. |
| **Root Cause** | The underlying reason the condition of the observation exists. This field is typically developed in discussion with management. |
| **Effect** | The risk or exposure created by the gap. What could happen or has happened, and to whom? Quantify where feasible; label estimates as estimates. |

Observations should be concise. With whole observations being around 150–250 words.

## Rating

The observation's `Risk Level` (High / Medium / Low) **is** the significance determination. It is retrospective: the urgency of remediation, reflecting impact, likelihood and compensating controls.

`Action Item Required` defaults from the Risk Level but can be manually overridden. Medium and High classifications default to an action item being required. Observations with Action Item's required are later validated on the engagement level.

## Approval and locking

Observations are approved **within the test**: once all of a test's observations are recorded, the list is submitted and approved by the CAE.

Observations stay editable through reporting — root cause and recommendation feasibility are developed with management and thus are allowed to be adjusted up-to the point of the report's issuance. Observations are locked at **final report issuance**: condition, criteria, root cause, effect, rating and recommendation. Linked action items and disposition remain trackable. A supervisor/CAE unlock exists for corrections. 

## Remediation

Remediation of Observations are tracked through the [Action Item](/action-items) case flow.
